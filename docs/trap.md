# Trap, as a genre spec

Genre conventions to write against and to put in the style prompt. They
are common knowledge, not measurements from this repo. Replace them as
renders teach otherwise.

## Sound

- **Tempo:** 130 to 160 bpm with a half-time feel, so it nods like 65 to 80.
  Put the number in the style prompt; engines respect it loosely.
- **Drums:** booming 808 bass with slides, rolling and stuttering hi-hats
  (triplet and 1/32 rolls), a hard clap or snare on beat three of the
  half-time bar.
- **Melody:** a sparse, looped minor-key motif: bells, piano, flute,
  plucked synth. The emptier the beat, the more the jokes land.
- **Comedy trap** works best played completely straight: a menacing beat
  under ridiculous words. The deadpan is the joke. Write "serious, dark,
  hard-hitting" into the style even though the song is funny.

## Structure

A starting point for a streaming-length song of about two and a half to three
minutes:

| Section | Bars | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 4 | ad-libs, a tag line, or a spoken skit |
| Hook | 8 | the whole thing, first, so the title lands in 15 seconds |
| Verse 1 | 16 | set-up jokes, escalating |
| Hook | 8 | |
| Verse 2 | 16 | the sharpest jokes |
| Bridge | 4 to 8 | a change of register: a spoken bit, a beat drop |
| Hook | 8 | optionally `[Hook (final)]` with a twist |
| Outro | 4 | the last joke, then the beat cuts |

For a short-form clip (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) the hook is the clip. Write it
so its first eight bars stand alone.

## Flow

- **Triplet flow** (three syllables per beat, "Migos flow" as a description,
  not a style-prompt term) packs jokes densely. Rest between bars so the
  punchline gets air.
- **Bar length:** about ten to sixteen syllables per bar at a half-time 140.
  `flow` shows the counts, so keep a verse's lines within a few syllables of
  each other unless a long line is deliberate.
- **End punchlines on the downbeat.** The last word of the bar is the
  loudest one.

## Ad-libs

They are written in parentheses on their own line or at the end of a line.
In comedy trap they are a second voice that comments on the first: "(it's
over)", "(refresh)", "(again?)". Engines may sing, drop or garble them, so
keep the important ones short.
