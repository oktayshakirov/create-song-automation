# Hooks

The hook is the song. A listener remembers one line, so the job is to make
that line both the joke and the thing they cannot stop singing.

These are working rules, not measurements. Revise them when a real render
proves one wrong.

## Rules

1. **The title is in the hook, and the hook starts with it or ends with it.**
2. **Short lines.** Aim for eight to twelve syllables per line; `check` warns
   past fourteen. A hook line has to survive being sung back after one listen.
3. **Repeat, then turn.** State the phrase, repeat it (exactly or with one
   word changed), then turn it on the last line. The punchline lives in the
   turn.
4. **The joke has to be true** to the experience. "Come back in five hours"
   is funny because everyone has seen it. Exaggerate the feeling, not the
   facts.
5. **One image, not three.** A hook that explains is a verse.
6. **Rhyme on open vowels** (ay, oh, ee). They sustain when sung, and AI
   vocals smear closed consonant rhymes.
7. **Leave gaps for ad-libs.** Trap hooks breathe. A gap at the end of each
   line is where "(skrrt)" or "(it's over)" lands, and it often gets the
   bigger laugh.
8. **Write it once.** Later `[Hook]` tags stay empty, so the export repeats it
   exactly. A hook that changes between choruses is a mistake, unless the last
   chorus has a deliberate twist. In that case, name it `[Hook (final)]` so
   the change is visible.

## Funny and catchy are different jobs

Catchy comes from repetition, short lines, and a melody that resolves. Funny
comes from surprise and truth. They pull against each other, because a joke
told four times stops being a surprise. So:

- **Keep the hook's joke a feeling**, not a twist: the relatable groan
  survives repetition.
- **Put the surprises in the verses** and the ad-libs, which are heard once.

## Riffing on a famous song

"Claude's Plan" as a pun on a famous title is fine. Echoing its lyric lines,
its melody or its signature hook rhythm is not. See `originality.md`, and put
the reference song's well-known lines in the sheet's `Banned:` field so
`check` stops them sneaking back in.
